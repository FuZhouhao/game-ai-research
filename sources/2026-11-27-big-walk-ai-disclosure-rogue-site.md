## 借鉴要点
- IGF（独立游戏节）评委集体发布声明：不为任何使用 AI 的游戏投票；随后被揭晓评委争议最大焦点之一就是 House House 的 Big Walk——一款 GOTY 竞争者、口碑极佳的游戏（rogue.site）。
- House House 向 Aftermath 披露的量化口径："estimate that well under 0.1% of Big Walk's code" 由生成式 AI 辅助；除此外游戏没有任何其他生成式 AI 使用。
- 关键披露链条：内部无法解决的技术问题 → 先咨询 Unity 工程师（也无法解决）→ 最后才咨询 chatbot、找到可行修复并加以修改。最终结果："a mere ten lines of code were partially generated by AI"——LLM 只是人类专家都失败后的最后手段调试工具。
- 量级极小（<0.1%、约 10 行代码）仍成为舆论风暴中心：说明舆论走向主要取决于披露语境（IGF 评委的立场声明）而非使用量本身；团队对外披露 AI 使用时应预期任何量级都可能被放大审视。
- HN 高赞主流观点（spicyusername，44 条子评论）："We will have to get used to judging software based on the output not on the input"——评判标准应从"投入了多少人力技艺"转向"产出是否独特、有趣、有创造力"。
- HN 有效区分框架（disillusionist，23 条子评论）："AI as tools" vs "AI as creative director"——用 AI 做重构/查 bug（glorified "find-and-replace"）与让 AI 当创意总监是两回事；并类比当年"Photoshop/数字媒介算不算真艺术"之争。该框架对团队制定内部 AI 使用边界直接可用。
- 反方提醒（xnorswap）：把"AI 代码"与"AI 美术资产"在 AI 声明里混为一谈是有害的——两者的争议烈度完全不同（HN 多人认为代码的使用争议远小于美术/文案，因为后者涉及未经授权的训练素材）。
- 可持续性追问（HN 讨论）：坚持 100% AI-free 标准的社区会不会变得 niche？以及反向案例（Coca Cola / McDonalds AI 广告）说明真正的否决标准应是"产出质量"而非"是否用过"（superultra）。

标题：How Big Walk inspired a new wave of debate on genAI in games
副题：Big Walk is now at the center of a big discourse
作者：Cass Marshall（Rogue，Editorial/Opinion，2026-11-12）

_Big Walk_ is an excellent game about wandering around a big island with your pals and solving puzzles from House House, creator of _Untitled Goose Game_. It's a Game of the Year contender and a delightfully silly experience that has earned its fair share of accolades. It's also the unexpected center of yet another debate about AI on social media.

The furor began when a group of jurors from the Independent Games Festival released a statement saying they would not vote for any title that used AI. At first, this seemed like a fairly standard stance; IGF's juror pool includes creatives, critics, and developers, all of whom tend to lean anti-AI for obvious reasons. Things got more complex on social media when it was eventually revealed one of the biggest topics of debate for jurors was _Big Walk_.

House House told Aftermath they "estimate that well under 0.1% of _Big Walk_'s code" was assisted by generative AI. There is no other use of generative AI used in the game, and an LLM was only used after House House encountered a technical issue that they could not internally solve. The developer then consulted an engineer at Unity, who was unable to come up with a solution. Finally, House House consulted a chatbot, eventually found a possible fix, and modified that answer. The end result is that a mere ten lines of code were partially generated by AI.

#### 热门评论摘录
> **spicyusername**（44 条子评论·热度第 1）：Eventually we'll get over having this discussion. AI is here and it trivializes what used to be hard. Developing software used to require feats of skill and endurance, and we're used to judging software based on that. Now it doesn't. We will have to get used to judging software based on the output not on the input. Whether what was made is unique, interesting, creative, not whether it took a lot of time or knowledge. There is no going back, only forward.

> **disillusionist**（23 条子评论·热度第 2）：My personal opinions on AI and AI usage in software development have fluctuated in the past year, especially as I've used it more in my professional work. As AI becomes more deeply embedded in the tools that I use, I struggle to identify a hard line on what "Made with AI (tm)" actually means . If I'm using it for refactoring work as basically a glorified "find-and-replace" or to help me track down the cause of tricky bugs through trace files, does that make my work "made with AI"? (I think "yes it does" and would follow up with "but does it matter?") If I send an agent to complete a JIRA ticket on a system that I designed, read the diff, and approve the changes - does that invalidate my prior design and development work? (I think "no".) If I use a website builder, a 3rd party library, or one more abstraction layer past what is currently acceptable, should that change how others or I feel about that project? I'm not really sure, but since I work on business software I don't think my customers really care about how the sausage is made; they're mostly looking for results. I do, however, see a massive difference between "AI as tools" and "AI as creative director". I agree with the mindset of "I want to hear stories from humans" and "I want to experience creative expression from humans". But some of the current discussion feels like the arguments we had back in the day of whether Photoshop/digital media/video games were "real art" or not. If a human has creative vision and uses a robot stir-er to mix their paints, I don't think that should invalidate their painting. We can still judge it, though.

> **superultra**（8 条子评论·热度第 4）：This article hooked me when it mentioned Big Wall, because Big Walk is easily one of the best games I've played in years. It sounds like this author's argument is "AI in games sucks for lots of reason but mostly because it robs people of jobs." I don't disagree with that in theory but the real question is whether House House would have actually hired another human. My guess is that they would've either delayed it more, or not known about the bugs and released it. They are purposefully small and bringing on even a freelancer introduces a lot of complexity and room for error. So too does AI but ultimately you control the output. You don't have to worry about it being late, or it having a bad day. If the end result of using AI is an artful, beautiful game like Big Wall, I'm all for it. In my opinion the metric shouldn't be whether AI was used, it should be whether the end result is *good*. I realize that's subjective but I think we can objectively say Big Walk is a wonderful, artful game. When AI is used and the output is bad - as was the case, say, with those Coca Cola or McDonalds ads - that's when we should question it. When it's being used for artwork or writing - I also think that's a valid argument against it because it is, as we all know, utilizing artwork and writing without consent. Is that true for code? Maybe some code but we would all say, I think, it's much looser around code than creative outputs like artwork and writing. It sounds like House House used AI like a tool the same way they did whatever engines or coding tools they used. The metric should be quality.
