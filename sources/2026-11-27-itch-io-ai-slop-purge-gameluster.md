itch.io deletes terabytes of AI slop as daily uploads double

November 3, 2026 by Cody Harwell

Indie game marketplace itch.io said on November 3, 2026 that it had deleted "a couple terabytes" of AI-generated content from its platform, following a two-month surge that roughly doubled the number of game pages created each day.

"Just deleted a couple terabytes of ai slop," the company wrote in a post on X.

## Uploads doubled, demand did not

Days before the purge, itch.io shared a graph on Bluesky showing that daily game page creations on the PC storefront rose from just over 3,000 on August 3, 2026 to above 6,000 by October 30. The company was blunt about what that volume was worth: downloads and purchases, it said, have not doubled.

When a follower asked what was behind the surge, the account replied, "Take a guess." itch.io says the increase is driven by first-time uploaders rather than bulk uploaders, and it confirmed that one user who flooded the site with near-identical AI-created games had those pages de-indexed from search, since bulk uploads violate its content guidelines.

## New disclosure rules and a search filter

Alongside the deletions, itch.io is expanding its generative AI disclosure requirements. The tabletop, books, comics and soundtracks categories now require creators to complete an AI disclosure to create or edit a page, joining the game assets section, which has required it for the past two years. The disclosure is required even if AI-generated elements were later edited out.

The quality guidelines that cover this behavior are not new. itch.io pointed to an AI spam clause that has been in its quality guidelines for over two years, barring uploads of excessive amounts of automatically generated content. itch.io has not said exactly which files the terabyte purge removed.

For readers, the practical change is filtering. itch.io says it is adding generative AI disclosure filtering to more places across the site where it was lacking, including the search results page, so users browsing games and assets can exclude AI-generated content from what they see.
